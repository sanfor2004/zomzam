# Positioning And Growth

## Positioning

Zomzam is the work-social operating system for builders who want to turn daily execution into visible reputation and paid opportunities.

Plain-English offer:

> Run your work, money, clients, messages, and professional network from one private dashboard with a public profile that shows momentum.

## Core Audience

Best early users:

- Freelance developers.
- Web designers.
- No-code builders.
- Small agencies.
- Students transitioning into paid client work.
- Technical creators who want a portfolio plus operations stack.

Avoid broad consumer productivity messaging at launch. It dilutes the product.

## Differentiators

- Public profile with the user's posts and connect/block social graph.
- Social feed built around asks, wins, and professional updates.
- Direct messages and friend graph.
- Time execution tied to delivery.
- Money ledger tied to client revenue.
- CRM lead pipeline tied to projects, tasks, and income.

## Guiding Metric

Use weekly connected builders as the guiding metric:

> Number of weekly active users who have at least one connection and perform at least one social or work action.

This is better than raw signups because the product only becomes valuable when social density forms.

## Growth Loops

### Profile Loop

User improves profile -> shares it -> someone visits -> sees posts/status/work -> connects or signs up.

Primary surfaces:

- `/u/[username]`
- public posts
- wins
- accepted answers

### Ask/Help Loop

User posts an ask -> qualified people are notified -> helper answers -> asker accepts -> helper receives reputation and notification -> more people post asks.

Primary surfaces:

- `PostComposer`
- ask posts
- comments
- `accept_answer`
- notifications

### Client Loop

User finds a lead -> qualifies it -> project/tasks/income are created -> user posts a win -> network sees proof -> referrals and inbound interest grow.

Primary surfaces:

- CRM map/leads/pipeline
- Money income
- Time tasks
- win posts

### Presence Loop

User sees active people -> opens chat -> gets response -> returns because the workspace feels alive.

Primary surfaces:

- right sidebar
- chat dock
- public profile status
- notifications

## Acquisition Channels

Ranked for early stage:

1. Direct founder outreach to freelancers and student builders.
2. Build-in-public posts showing real workflows, not generic screenshots.
3. Small community seeding in Discord, WhatsApp, Facebook groups, and local tech communities.
4. Portfolio/profile sharing loop.
5. Lead-generation/CRM content for freelancers.
6. Launch platforms only after retention loops work.

## First 90 Days

### Weeks 1-3: Founding Circle

Goal: 20-50 trusted users.

Actions:

- Recruit manually.
- Watch onboarding sessions.
- Fix profile, feed, messaging, and CRM friction immediately.
- Push users to connect with each other, not just create accounts.

### Month 1: One Niche Density

Goal: one tight group where users recognize each other.

Actions:

- Focus on freelance web builders.
- Create sample public profiles.
- Encourage ask/win posting.
- Turn useful asks into visible proof.

### Month 2: Controlled Expansion

Goal: 250-500 users with real social graph formation.

Actions:

- Add one adjacent niche.
- Publish case studies: "from lead to task to income to win post."
- Improve invite/share loops.

### Month 3: Launch Spike

Goal: external launch only when retention exists.

Actions:

- Public launch campaign.
- Founder demo videos.
- Community challenges around profiles, wins, and asks.
- Announce CRM/pro direction without forcing payment too early.

## Messaging Pillars

- Own your work OS.
- Turn your profile into proof.
- Turn help into reputation.
- Turn leads into tracked revenue.
- Keep execution, money, and clients connected.
